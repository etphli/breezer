**Overall Safari visual design quality: 8.6/10.**

Safari’s visible interface is highly polished, with precise alignment, restrained typography and a clear separation between browser controls and page content. Its strongest quality is compositional discipline. Toolbars, sidebars, cards and popovers each have a coherent structure without demanding excessive attention.

The main weakness is visual legibility at the quieter end of that system. Pale controls, subtle selection fills and translucent backgrounds sometimes make boundaries harder to read. Compact tabs and narrow reading-list rows also sacrifice useful text to truncation. These are meaningful deductions from an otherwise accomplished design.

I inspected all six supplied images and read `manifest.json`. They are official vendor references from [Apple’s Safari page](https://www.apple.com/safari/), not live app captures. Website artwork, photography, desktop decoration and device industrial design contribute nothing to these scores.

The evidence supports these observations:

- **`hero.jpg`** shows a well-organized start page and a privacy report with clear summary blocks. Reading cards contain substantial small text, while section labels sit directly over a busy background.
- **`customization.jpg`** shows disciplined toolbar and tab-strip composition. Its dominant green page and editorial typography belong to the website. It does **not** show the start-page customization controls.
- **`profiles.png`** shows a conspicuous, labeled profile pill. The inactive window’s faded appearance is state-dependent and is not evidence that active controls are universally low contrast.
- **`tab-groups.png`** provides the strongest navigation evidence. Indentation, section labels and the selected group make the structure readable. Long nested page names still truncate.
- **`privacy-report.jpg`** presents the site, tracker count and domain list clearly. The large summary and generous spacing help comprehension, although the disclosure symbol and separators are visually faint.
- **`reading-list.jpg`** shows consistent saved-item anatomy across the depicted devices. The desktop sidebar’s limited width constrains title recognition. The article’s typography and photography are website content.

My confidence is **high in the visible compositional judgments and moderate in their coverage of Safari overall**. The 8.6 is an overall judgment of the app design represented by this set, rather than a score for the marketing imagery or a complete product audit.

Settings, dark mode, responsive behavior, motion, interaction and native runtime behavior remain **unverified**. Separate device images establish visible layouts, not how those layouts adapt or behave. Cropping, overlap, image scaling and curated content also limit assessment of crowded windows, long lists and less favorable background choices.
