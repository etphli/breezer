**Overall Safari design quality: 8.6/10.**

Safari’s visible interface is highly polished, with disciplined alignment, restrained typography and a clear separation between browsing controls and page content. Its main weakness is legibility: pale controls, translucent backgrounds and compact labels sometimes make the interface less immediately readable than its clean composition suggests.

I inspected all six supplied images and read `manifest.json`. These are official [Apple Safari product references](https://www.apple.com/safari/), not live app captures. This score judges the Safari interface represented across the set. Website typography, photography, illustrations, desktop wallpaper and device industrial design earn no credit.

The strongest work is structural. In `customization.jpg`, the toolbar groups controls without filling the whole width with equally prominent buttons. In `tab-groups.png`, section headings, indentation and a selected row make the relationship between groups and their tabs understandable from a still image. In `profiles.png`, the named profile pill supplies an explicit identity cue alongside its symbol and color.

The finish is convincing, but visual quietness occasionally goes too far. The toolbar’s pale boundaries offer limited emphasis, and the selected tab in `customization.jpg` is distinguished fairly gently. The forest-backed start page demonstrates the risk of placing white labels directly over detailed imagery. The photograph itself is excluded from the score; Safari’s treatment of interface text over it is relevant.

The privacy presentation is particularly clear. `privacy-report.jpg` gives the site, tracker count and explanation distinct roles, then uses simple separators for the domain list. The larger report in `hero.jpg` also separates summary metrics from detail effectively. Reading list is less resolved: the desktop sidebar squeezes titles and descriptions into narrow rows, while the phone presentation in `reading-list.jpg` devotes space to recently saved cards above overlapping list content.

Confidence is **moderately high for the visible visual judgment**, with narrower coverage of Safari as a complete product. Cropping, device overlap, image scaling and vendor-selected examples conceal ordinary edge cases. The set shows several device layouts but does not verify responsive behavior.

Settings, dark mode, motion, interaction, keyboard focus, hover states and native runtime behavior are **unverified**. The customization controls and profile-switching workflows are also unverified. Those limits constrain what this review establishes without preventing an overall judgment of the visible design.
