Collect little. Leave your browsing alone.

Breezer is an open-source browser built on Helium and Chromium, maintained by Ethan. Our usage statistics measure how much the browser is used without recording what you do on the web. We do not sell usage reports, use them for targeted advertising, or build a browsing profile from them.

This policy covers Breezer's own website and usage reporting. Websites you visit and services you choose to connect have their own privacy practices. You can inspect the source to see how Breezer's reporting works.

What stays on your Mac

Your browser profile holds data such as history, bookmarks, saved passwords, tabs, spaces, and appearance preferences locally. Basic usage reporting does not upload that data. Breezer does not require a Breezer account to browse.

Private windows are excluded from Breezer's usage measurements. Private browsing does not make you invisible to websites, your network provider, or services you deliberately use.

Small measurements. No browsing diary.

Versions of Breezer that include usage reporting enable it by default. The report contains only a random monthly identifier and daily totals of active-use seconds. We use these to measure active browser profiles and time spent using Breezer. The identifier changes each calendar month and is not derived from your hardware, email address, or account.

Time is estimated in one-minute samples while a regular Breezer window is focused and your computer is active. Sleeping, idle, background, and private windows do not contribute. Reports are sent when you are active, at most once every 30 minutes after the first report, over HTTPS.

Reports contain no visited URLs, searches, page contents, screenshots, passwords, AI messages, email addresses, hardware identifiers, or precise location. These limited reports are pseudonymous, not a promise of absolute anonymity. Hosting providers receive network information such as your IP address when delivering a request.

We count monthly active browser profiles in the current calendar month and active hours over the last 30 UTC days. These are measurements of reporting profiles, not verified unique people. Older released versions do not report these statistics.

You have the switch.

Open Breezer Settings → Privacy and usage and turn off Share basic usage statistics. This stops future reports, cancels any pending request where possible, and clears the local usage counter. You can keep using Breezer with reporting off.

Disabling reporting does not retract a request that has already arrived. Existing reports remain subject to the retention period below. Re-enabling reporting creates a fresh random identifier. Clearing your browser profile also resets the local identifier.

This setting governs Breezer's own usage reports. It does not disable the network requests needed to load websites, check for updates, download releases, or use services you choose to connect. GitHub's aggregate installer download count is separate.

Connected only when you choose.

The AI sidebar connects to your signed-in Codex or Claude CLI. When you ask it to work with a page, relevant page context, screenshots, messages, and tool results may be sent to that provider. These are assistant requests, not usage analytics. Your selected provider's terms and privacy policy apply.

Breezer's personal context and conversation features keep local files for the assistant and may supply that context to the selected provider. Optional Organato features contact Organato to retrieve or change the items you request. Organato credentials are stored in macOS Keychain. Extensions you install and websites you visit may collect their own data.

Review what you share with any assistant or connected service. You can disconnect optional services without giving up ordinary browsing.

A website without an advertising tracker.

Our website does not add advertising pixels, session replay, analytics cookies, or a visitor fingerprint. Vercel hosts the website and usage endpoint and may process IP addresses, request metadata, security events, and operational logs to deliver and protect them.

GitHub hosts the source and installers and maintains aggregate asset download counts. Our private dashboard reads installer counts from GitHub. A download is a file transfer, not proof of an installation or a unique person. See Vercel's privacy policy and GitHub's privacy statement.

Less collected. Less to protect.

Usage reports are stored in a private Vercel Blob store. The dashboard requires Vercel authentication and project access. Storage credentials stay on the server. We retain reports for the current and previous calendar month, with a scheduled daily cleanup, so reports expire within 63 days. Operational logs are governed separately by the hosting provider's retention settings.

Service providers may process data in countries outside your own. We do not intentionally add sensitive browsing information to reports. No network or storage system can promise perfect security, and local profile protection also depends on the security of your Mac.

Questions and privacy requests

Depending on where you live, you may have rights to access, correct, delete, or object to the processing of personal information. Contact the maintainer through the Breezer repository to ask a privacy question or arrange a suitable way to make a request. Do not post passwords, browsing history, identifiers, or other private information in a public issue.

We do not collect names or contact information in usage reports, so we may be unable to associate a report with a person. We do not knowingly collect personal information from children through usage reporting. If you believe sensitive or children's information has reached us, contact the maintainer.

Changes you can read

We will update this page and its revision date when these practices change. Material changes to reporting will also be explained in the relevant release notes. This policy describes Breezer's practices; it does not replace the policies of websites or providers you choose to use.
