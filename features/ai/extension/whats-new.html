A setup guide for new profiles

A short guide helps you choose tabs, appearance, Chrome import and an optional Organato connection.

Skip any step, or reopen the guide in Settings.

Open the setup guide
Setup choices

A new profile now opens a short setup guide instead of these notes. Pick the name Breezer AI greets you by, where your tabs sit, a theme color and appearance, then import from Chrome and connect Organato if you use it. A quick tour of the sidebar and the assistant finishes it, and you can skip at any step.

Already set up? Open the guide whenever you like with the Setup guide button in Settings.

The Organato step has a one-click Don't use Organato button. It saves your choice before moving on, keeps Organato off when you return, and shows Turned off in your setup summary.

Shortcuts you choose

Add a website or an open tab to your new-tab shortcuts. Command-number also opens saved favorites and pins.

Keyboard and saved shortcuts

Top tabs, private windows and Command+9 keep their usual numbered-tab behavior. A slot without a saved favorite or pin falls back to the ordinary tab shortcut.

On a new tab, the Add tile after your sites takes a web address or one of your open tabs. Arrow keys move through the tabs and Enter adds the one you pick. Your shortcuts are saved in this profile on your Mac, and they use the same Remove, Undo and Restore controls as the suggested sites. On a smaller window, the row makes room for them.

For sidebar favorites and pins, Command-number shortcuts now open the saved page even when it is asleep. Only the selected page opens, repeated presses reuse it, and favorite slots stay consistent across spaces and restarts.

Tasks open where they live

Open assignments from Upcoming with one click. Turn Organato off for a new tab with only the date and shortcuts.

When off, new tabs make no Organato data requests.

Organato behavior

Assignment titles in the new tab's Upcoming list now link to that task's own page in Organato, so one click takes you to the details.

With Organato turned off, new tabs show just the date and your shortcuts, with no empty Organato panel or reserved space. Open new tabs update straight away, and the choice survives reloads and restarts.

Those new tabs do not start the Organato helper or request Organato data while it is off. Turning it back on restores the panel and fetches fresh data.

No more ghost tabs

Closed tabs stay closed, and newer titles and selections take priority.

Sidebar update details

A tab you closed could come back to the sidebar when an older list of tabs arrived late, sometimes with an outdated title or the wrong tab highlighted. The sidebar now ignores lists older than what it already shows.

  • Restoring a large saved session also takes less work. Matching saved tabs and preparing the sidebar no longer repeatedly scan the whole saved workspace.
  • When one tab changes, the rows that didn't are left as they are. Keyboard focus stays on the existing row instead of being lost to a redraw.

Helpers that rest when idle

AI, Organato and update helpers start when needed and close when idle. Your next action reconnects automatically.

Update verification and rollback checks stay in place. Interrupted changes are not resent automatically.

Helper lifecycle
  • The background helpers behind Breezer AI, Organato and updates now start when something needs them and close once they've been idle, rather than staying open.
  • Organato requests from Settings and new tabs share a helper. Each page keeps its own requests, so closing one page does not cancel another page's work.
  • Breezer AI loads its provider code with your first request. Opening Settings or serving Organato requests does not load that code just in case.
  • After Settings has sat quietly for a while, your next action reconnects on its own. What you typed stays in place, and an interrupted change is never resent automatically.
  • An update you've checked for stays ready to install. If the connection drops first, Breezer asks you to check again. The checksum, identity, signature and rollback checks are unchanged.

Less work at startup, less left on disk

Breezer can reuse prepared block lists and clean up copies left by ended sessions.

Running copies, older versions and copies with unknown owners are kept.

Startup and cleanup
  • Breezer can reuse prepared versions of unchanged built-in ad-block lists. Custom filters, list updates and your blocking settings work as before.
  • If a prepared list has changed or cannot be used, Breezer processes it normally instead.
  • While Breezer runs, it keeps a private copy of itself so macOS can still check its signature after an update. Copies left by sessions that have ended are now removed.
  • A copy a running Breezer still holds is never touched, and copies from earlier versions, or whose owner can't be confirmed, are kept.

Clearer passkey settings

The Passwords page explains storage and opens the local page to check passkey availability.

Touch ID and Apple Passwords remain unavailable. Saving passwords and CSV import work as before.

Password and passkey details

Touch ID and Apple Passwords remain unavailable until Breezer has the required Apple signing entitlements. Saving passwords and importing them from CSV work as before.

Password suggestions close in Chromium's usual way again as you type. The Microsoft sign-in Enter behavior remains unconfirmed, as noted below.

Research across tabs you choose

Choose source tabs in the assistant to compare and summarize up to six pages, with source references and clear text-only sharing. Research sources never enable browser control.

Source and sharing details

Review the selected pages before sharing their text. Source labels, URLs, captured lengths, truncation and individual page errors stay visible. Remove a source, clear the list or cancel a pending capture whenever you need to.

Translate a page, keep the original

Right-click a page and choose Translate page. Read in another language, then restore the original at any time.

Requires your LibreTranslate server or key. Breezer asks before sharing page text.

Translation options

Translate page text in place, choose languages and restore the original. Connect your local LibreTranslate server or use your own LibreTranslate.com key, with explicit confirmation before sharing page text.

Right-click a page and choose Translate page to begin. Translation needs your provider to be running; Breezer does not bundle translation models or use your AI account.

Stop, retry or restore the original text. Optional translation of new text stays within the chosen page while its translation window is open. Links, form values and the page's existing controls are retained.

Find settings by category or search

Search by name, description or keyword. Press / to start, or choose a category. All settings opens the full browser settings.

Navigation and appearance

Settings has a new look. Categories sit in a list on the left, and search takes you straight to the setting you need.

Press / to search by name, description or keyword. Search also offers Chromium's own settings. Choose All settings to open the full browser settings page.

Back and Forward move between categories. Use the keyboard to move through categories and search results; the layout follows your Breezer theme and light or dark appearance.

Performance measurements

Browser and saved-tab tests, including slower results

Measured against Chrome and the previous Breezer

We timed a pre-release Breezer 1.2.0 build beside the previous Breezer and Chrome 156. Two measurements improved in this build, and two leads over Chrome carried over from before. See the full results, including slower and unavailable measurements.

New in this build

First local page after tab exercises

63.5% less time than the previous Breezer. After each launch the test opens and switches tabs, then loads a small local page three times. The first of those loads took 262.2 ms instead of 717.4 ms (medians of 6 launches). Chrome took 52.4 ms on the same workload, so it is still quicker here.

  • Breezer 1.2.0 candidate 262.2 ms
  • Previous Breezer 717.4 ms
  • Chrome 156 52.4 ms

Restoring saved-tab records

Restoring 1,000 records took 2.90 ms instead of 189.96 ms. A separate synthetic test of the sidebar’s workspace code compares the previous Breezer code with the 1.2.0 candidate code. Building the sidebar model for the same records took 0.187 ms instead of 3.299 ms. It times this code alone, not 1,000 open tabs or overall browser speed.

More browser measurements

Still ahead of Chrome, as before

New tab: 50.3% less time than Chrome

Breezer’s own new-tab page was ready in 98.8 ms, against 198.7 ms for Chrome’s (medians of 18 tabs). The previous Breezer measured 99.2 ms, so this lead isn’t new in 1.2.

  • Breezer 1.2.0 candidate 98.8 ms
  • Previous Breezer 99.2 ms
  • Chrome 156 198.7 ms

Relaunch: 19.5% less time than Chrome

Quitting and reopening a set-up test profile until the page was usable took 861.3 ms, against 1,070.2 ms for Chrome (medians of 6 launches). The previous Breezer measured 846.6 ms, so this lead isn’t new either.

  • Breezer 1.2.0 candidate 861.3 ms
  • Previous Breezer 846.6 ms
  • Chrome 156 1,070.2 ms

How this was measured, and what it doesn’t show

  • One Apple M1 Mac with 8 GB of memory, macOS 26.7, on AC power with no thermal warning. 6 rounds rotated the launch order: 36 timed launches after 18 untimed setup launches, and all 36 timed launches passed their foreground check.
  • Startups use test profiles that were already set up, then reused. They aren’t cold starts after a reboot, and no caches were flushed.
  • Page loads use the same small local test page in every browser, not real websites over the internet.
  • Process-tree RSS adds up the resident memory of every browser process and can count shared memory more than once. It isn’t the memory Activity Monitor shows. Physical footprint is the memory macOS charges to those processes, including compressed memory.
  • Chrome’s physical footprint is unavailable: 11 of its 60 samples failed process-identity or process-tree checks, and the remaining samples aren’t summarized on their own.
  • CPU use couldn’t be measured: in all 36 sampling windows the CPU time stayed within the counters’ uncertainty. These results make no CPU, energy or battery-life claims.
  • Six rounds give medians and ranges, not statistically significant differences.
  • The candidate is a private ad hoc signed build of the combined 1.2.0 source (snapshot fa19c37); its internal version still reads 0.20.1.1. Its release identity and full feature checks are still pending, so the published release may measure differently.

Update 1.2.1

Less repeated work

Breezer keeps unchanged sidebar rows, avoids rescanning translated text, and preserves completed reply text.

These changes reduce repeated work in the checked components. Whole-browser RAM and CPU savings have not yet been measured. Existing translation sharing, browser permissions and update verification remain in place.

Engineering details
  • Sidebar membership updates avoid reading and serializing saved tabs. Nonvisual tab changes avoid an unnecessary sidebar redraw.
  • Unchanged sync recovery data is no longer written repeatedly. Disposed sync clients cannot recreate their repeating alarm.
  • Translation rescans skip repeated visibility measurements for unchanged translated text.
  • Streamed replies keep completed content in place. Native helper messages avoid redundant copying and repeated parsing, and cancelled requests release setup work sooner.

Known limitations

Encrypted sync

Encrypted two-profile and password-sync checks remain pending. Passkeys are excluded from sync.

Details

Native encrypted-vault setup, provider transport and the saved-workspace adapter are included in the combined source. Encrypted two-profile and password-sync checks remain pending. Passkeys are excluded from sync.

Google Docs editing

Typing, editing and saved changes still need real-document checks with both AI providers.

Details

Added a bounded Google Docs editor input path; real-document verification pending. Typing, selection, follow-up editing and saved changes still need checks in the compiled browser with both AI providers.

Enter on Microsoft sign-in

Enter on Microsoft's password step remains unconfirmed.

Details

Password suggestions close in Chromium's usual way again as you type. This hasn't yet been confirmed to fix pressing Enter on Microsoft's password step.

A narrow Enter fallback is included for explicit username/current-password forms. Native password-picker, IME, validation and site checks remain pending.

Touch ID passkeys

Touch ID passkeys are unavailable without Apple-approved signing entitlements. Saving and filling passwords are separate.

Details

Website passkey sign-in with Touch ID still needs Apple-approved signing entitlements that Breezer does not have yet. This is separate from saving and filling passwords.
